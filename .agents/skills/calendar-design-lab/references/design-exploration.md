# Design exploration, revision and learning

Use this reference when a calendar brief needs visual exploration or a selected direction needs revision. Keep the task boundary: a discussion of product capabilities needs analysis; an actual design commission needs visible work. Do not turn the former into an unsolicited calendar or questionnaire.

Use [discovery](discovery.md) for important unknowns and the [unified atlas](../../../../docs/design-atlas.md) for linked structures, rules, studies and material candidates. Detailed design reasoning remains in [design-language.md](design-language.md).

## Turn an interpretation into a direction

Start with the user's use and the theme's proposed role. Separate confirmed requirements from defaults and exploratory hypotheses. A theme can affect structure, typography, imagery, content or interaction; its name does not determine a palette or illustration style.

For each direction, retain a compact card with the decisions needed to make and assess it:

| Decision | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Reading task | What the person should see or do first, and in what setting |
| Theme interpretation | How the theme participates; the evidence or hypothesis behind that choice |
| Time and structure | Actual period, granularity, mapping, boundaries, spatial organization and indexes |
| Main relationship | The composition's organizing idea: sequence, image/date relationship, writing area or another explicit relationship |
| Type and image roles | Display, lookup, caption, main image and secondary content; selected files or clearly marked placeholders |
| Whitespace | What each substantial empty area serves, such as writing, grouping or viewing pause |
| Fixed and variable | Relationships to preserve within a series or revision, and what may change |
| Evidence and limits | Linked rules/material IDs, retained source/preview, checks and what still needs user evaluation |

Let the card follow the actual design rather than filling every cell mechanically. A material from the atlas is a candidate until selected; an old sample's original record remains authoritative about its actual assets.

For the first small study, a minimal working record is enough: request, confirmed constraints, explicit provisional choices, the direction's main hypothesis and actual source/preview paths. Add detail as choices become material or a revision is selected. Do not postpone a useful first artifact to complete every design-card field.

For the hypothetical request “书籍主题的周历,” possible interpretations include a publication-like weekly page, a book-centered viewing page, or a reading record. These are hypotheses for that brief, not three mandatory templates. A chapter heading might organize a week, marginal notes might support reflection, or a licensed image might anchor a page. Do not assume old paper, a particular book, or a wish to track reading.

## Make the comparison visible

Choose the least expensive artifact that can answer the current design question: a composition sketch, a type/date crop, a representative page or a short adjacent-page sequence. When the user requests design work, show actual visible studies with enough real content to judge the proposed relationship; prose describing styles alone is insufficient.

- A **whole direction** may change structure, typography, imagery and proportions together. Explain the idea and tradeoffs without claiming one variable caused the user's reaction.
- A **controlled comparison** keeps stated controls and changes the variable under investigation, such as date mapping or writing-area allocation.
- An already precise brief may need one well-developed draft. An uncertain brief may benefit from a few distinct directions; do not impose a fixed count or generate cosmetic variations merely to fill options.

Label sample dates, provisional output dimensions and placeholders. Keep dates deterministic. Do not fabricate book quotations, author facts, user events, progress or claimed rights to a cover. For a proposed series, retain the planned content/date mapping and distinguish a representative page from a complete series.

Inspect the actual rendered artifact before presenting it. Check the intended language and content, not only the example numerals. G/J5 are retained weekly studies, not a general book-calendar engine; current English proofs do not establish Chinese layout support. Resolve a necessary rendering gap or state the limited preview scope rather than silently changing the requested language.

Present the preview explicitly in a user-facing message before asking the person to compare directions. An inspection tool's image output is not evidence that the person has seen the design. Use an inline image or an accessible preview panel/link, with clear direction labels; if delivery failed, show the artifacts before collecting design preferences.

## Convert feedback into a scoped revision

Locate the referenced version in the active case, current files or attachments before asking the user to provide it again. If the parent is unavailable, ask only for the missing source or preview needed to edit accurately, while retaining the stated keep/change intent. Do not substitute G/J5 or a synthetic example for the user's unidentified design.

Record the affected direction/revision and separate:

- **Keep:** the relationships the user explicitly wants retained, with their scope.
- **Change:** the requested outcome and the specific design decisions it affects.
- **Unknown:** ambiguities in the feedback or effects that have not yet been checked.

For example, “保留七天的排法，图片小一点，多一些写字位置” supports retaining the seven-day arrangement while revising image scale and writing area. It does not establish a permanent dislike of large images. “更有书籍感” could concern content, type, page structure or materials; use a focused question or comparison when those interpretations would produce materially different edits.

Save the parent revision, feedback evidence, interpretation, changed parameters/source and the new preview. Preserve selected relationships unless the user's new instruction changes them. If an edit forces another relationship to change, make that consequence visible. A user may combine parts of directions; record which decisions were selected instead of pretending an untouched original was chosen.

The revised output starts as unreviewed even when it retains approved parts. Carry forward the supported decisions, not blanket approval of every new output. Record technical checks and the user's design judgement separately using [review.md](review.md).

## Keep three kinds of evidence distinct

| Activity | What it can establish |
| --- | --- |
| Research conversation | Reported behavior, context, meanings and stated needs; not proof that a proposed layout works |
| Co-design and actual use | Choices, localized feedback, revision results and use problems within the observed situation |
| Technical verification | Date/content correctness, glyphs, bounds, retained assets and demonstrated replay scope; not personal satisfaction |

Before treating a design as a reusable selected recipe, retain its actual source, assets, parameters and evidence as described in [recipe.md](recipe.md). Do not call a prompt alone a saved design, or a successful replay evidence that another person will like it.

## Decide what to learn from a case

| Finding | Next home |
| --- | --- |
| An individual's taste or routine | That case's preference record, scoped to the evidence |
| A repeated design relationship with an explainable use condition | A conditional skill rule linked to cases, tradeoffs and counterexamples |
| A stable, recurring operation with clear inputs and outputs | A reusable tool, checked against the demonstrated failure or requirement |
| Conflicting outcomes or unclear causes | An unresolved hypothesis and a useful next comparison |

A case count is a planning aid, not an automatic promotion threshold. Before broadening a rule, seek another relevant context and inspect where it fails. Keep the original observation separate from the proposed transfer. Prefer a narrow correction supported by actual behavior to adding general rules for every reaction.

End a case update with what was produced, what changed and stayed, what was checked, what the user actually accepted and what remains unresolved. This learning loop improves the workflow; it does not certify a universal design capability.
